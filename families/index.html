The six harness families

The decision points every coding-agent turn carries — visibility, permissions, tool fit, routing, sensitivity, cache reuse — measured on our own fixture populations, our lanes only. This section is deliberately quarantined from the main benchmark board: its data file (data/families.json) never enters the board's areas, cc or index math.

Honest caveat — self-authored fixtures. These six families are our own synthetic decision-point fixtures, not standardized external benchmarks. Gates prevent train/eval token leakage, but the question styles, distractors and class balance are ours. These numbers measure how our lanes behave on our own fixture distribution — engineering signal, not capability claims. Not comparable to the dataset-suite board or to the Jev Decision Index.

The eval populations are wide and template-disjoint — 96–100 authored cases per family (class-balanced, zero shared 3-grams with the family's corpus or calibration texts, per-family label-token bans, BLAKE3 digest pins), with harness_cache_reuse keeping its frozen 12-case record (the documented divergence). The page shows each family's chance tier beside the accuracies: the wide eval removed the old populations' corpus-vocabulary overlap, so these numbers are the honest, un-inflated reads — and they are low by design. The lanes:

Reflex (modelless) — the engine's run() baseline posture on the wide eval (count tables off, fused-gate thresholds fitted at the cal 30th percentile). Rethink (hybrid) — the A0 seat posture through reflex's own seat seam (no specialist artifacts exist on these families — the served arm IS the modelless tier, with the seat's count-table + gate fit on top). Rethink (encoder) — requires trained heads on the family corpora; that is an unmade owner call, so the cell renders not run. Absence is honest, not failure.
